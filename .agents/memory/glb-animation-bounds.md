---
name: GLB animation bounds
description: Preserve animated frame-zero transforms during GLB sizing and verify upright posture in all axes.
---

Apply the first GLB animation frame before measuring bounds, and keep those transforms applied through `Box3.expandByObject(object, true)`. Do not call `AnimationMixer.stopAllAction()` first; it restores the unanimated transform values and can produce incorrect bounds. For animation regressions, test in the same prepare → clone → play order as production: bones not keyed by the next clip retain the prepared pose.

For upright-pose checks, measure the full 3D Hips-to-Head axis: `atan2(hypot(horizontalX, horizontalZ), verticalY)`. Testing only the character-right component misses forward/backward pitch, which can appear to switch screen sides when the actor turns around. On the Quaternius player GLB, neutralize the locomotion spine chain's average rotation and add neutral tracks for unanimated bones so Idle, Walk, and Run do not inherit tilted bind transforms. Preserve per-cycle motion and avoid a permanent rotation on the whole model.

Neutral quaternion tracks must span the full clip duration. A one-key track at time zero only clears a baseline pose at startup; with the production per-frame baseline restore, the source clip's tilted pose can leak back on later frames. Apply this to both bones absent from a clip and upright bones with a single keyed rotation.

The player's visible face is on the source GLB's native +Z side. The actor's π model-axis correction turns that front toward world -Z, so travel root yaw must align the corrected front with movement `(velocityX, -velocityY)`. Do not apply a second 180° turn based on assuming the source already faces -Z; test actual forward, backward, left, and right headings against the GLB's visible face.

Keep the player root's initial travel heading at zero and ease directly toward the movement heading. During normal Walk/Run clips, preserve the GLB's own lower-leg and foot motion instead of layering procedural ankle movement on top. Check straight travel and all four movement headings after any pose change.

Treat crouching as a full human squat, not just a pelvis drop: bend at the hips and knees, hinge the torso forward, counter the head, and preserve foot contact. Keep ordinary walking upright side-to-side without an added run lean. Check the real skinned bounds against the floor and the road's 1.4 m low beam, including with reduced motion enabled.

**Why:** A synthetic animated asset showed that stopping its mixer reset the mesh scale before fitting, so the size calculation measured the bind pose instead of the requested first animation frame. The player Run clip also omitted a Body rotation track; the prepared Idle pose therefore persisted into Run and caused about 14° of real lateral lean, while tests that played Run on the untouched GLB missed it. A separate production-order test found that one-key neutral tracks allowed the original tilted source pose to return after the first frame. The player also needs a clearly human squat rather than a small pelvis-only shift. Later screenshots exposed a separate forward-pitch component that lateral-only measurements could not detect. A heading test also assumed a native -Z face even though the source GLB face features sit at +Z; the extra 180° made the character face opposite its actual movement.

**How to apply:** In any GLB loading or fitting path, measure after a stable Idle frame is applied and verify returned world dimensions with an animated fixture. Neutralized quaternion tracks must cover the clip's full duration. For locomotion pose checks, reproduce the production initialization and clip transitions on a fresh asset, then measure both lateral lean and the complete 3D body axis across multiple frames. For crouching, test the actual animated GLB's full bounds at floor level and below the 1.4 m beam, with and without reduced motion. For facing checks, derive the native front from visible geometry, apply the model-axis correction once, and verify headings against real travel vectors. For feet, check the actual clip-driven pose and ground contact before adding procedural rotations.
