---
name: War Room camera framing
description: The War Room must remain third-person with the player visible.
---

The War Room always uses third-person framing with the player's back toward the camera; do not restore first-person from legacy preferences or show the player's front. Touch drags outside the movement joystick must never control the camera. Keep the camera on the player's rear-facing line; when geometry blocks that path, shorten the distance rather than moving to the side or front. When not aiming or in a brief post-attack strafe, face the player toward joystick movement and follow that body yaw from behind; aiming may retain camera-facing strafe. When the player is inside a building, keep the camera and look target inside that building, use wider framing than the old 2 m close-up but closer than outdoors, and shorten below preferred distance rather than pushing through a wall. Recover the player to a clear nearby position if they enter blocking geometry.

**Why:** The user explicitly requires a rear third-person view, movement-aligned turning, and readable interiors. They also reported that touching/dragging the screen changed the view and asked for the camera to stay behind the player. The bundled hero's corrected model axis uses the opposite Three.js root-yaw sign from gameplay heading, so treating those values as identical can place the camera in front of the visible character.

**How to apply:** Preserve third-person rear framing; keep the camera at least 2.5 metres away outdoors when clear. Movement-facing must be the default. Do not attach free-look behavior to world or action-button drags; only the movement joystick changes player heading. Convert gameplay yaw when applying it to the rendered hero root, then convert back for camera placement and turn-error checks. Keep camera placement on the rear line, using collision shortening when blocked instead of lateral or elevated detours. Indoor placement must honor swept wall clearance even when that makes the camera closer than its preferred distance; keep framing wide enough to show the player and room.
