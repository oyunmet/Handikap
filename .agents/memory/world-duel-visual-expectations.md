---
name: World and duel visual expectations
description: Player-facing requirements for stable world rivals, clear duels, and discoverable road rewards and hazards.
---

World encounter rivals must stay anchored to their encounter locations instead of pacing, following the player, or sliding when revisiting. During a duel, both fighters must face each other and appear side by side across the screen, not stacked vertically. Use a rear-side camera angle that keeps the player's weapon hand and attack effects clearly visible; never present the player facing the camera. The duel player must retain the same recognizable appearance as the road hero, not switch to a different-looking procedural figure. Keep bosses on a player-like humanoid rig and close to the player's height; distinguish them with polished, well-proportioned armor, silhouette, patterns, and shapes rather than making the hero look miniature beside an oversized boss or using a mismatched standalone skeleton. Attach weapons and equipment to the rig's hand and bones. Keep the arena clear of world scenery and duplicate overlays so both fighters remain visible. Road rewards and hazards must be visually obvious and reachable; seeded content that players cannot see does not satisfy the requirement.

Rig-attached equipment must account for the mount bone's full world scale after fitting, including nested GLB armature scales. Counter-scale both the attachment geometry and its local offsets, then check the combined model bounds and ground clearance in world and duel contexts.

The player hero GLB faces local -Z. The WorldV2 model-axis correction must remain additive to actor movement heading; subtracting the correction again cancels it and can turn forward/sideways travel into a visibly wrong facing direction.

**Why:** The user reported that a pushed change still left moving rivals, obscured duel fighters, and no visible treasure or traps, then clarified both the rear-camera requirement and that Demir Taç should resemble the player while remaining clearly distinct. They also contrasted the attractive road hero with the visibly poor duel figure and asked that the same hero carry into combat while Demir Taç looks polished like other rivals. A mobile duel screenshot later showed the player reading as miniature beside an oversized boss, so keep their proportions close and make identity differences through equipment. Later mobile screenshots showed the hero still facing incorrectly after torso lean had been removed; the remaining error was the model-axis yaw being canceled at the actor root.

**How to apply:** For changes to world actors, combat cameras, equipment, or pickups, check the rendered mobile scene as well as the data and movement code. Keep the road hero and duel player visually consistent; keep duel bosses near player scale and distinguish them with cohesive, scale-fitted rig-mounted gear. Project combatants horizontally with the camera over the player's weapon-side shoulder, apply both x and z actor coordinates, and align hit feedback with their screen positions. Test that attached weapons move with the hand during attacks. When the source rig has nested scaling, use the mount's world scale rather than only the scene root scale when sizing and positioning generated gear. For travel facing, transform the model's native -Z front axis through the wrapper and root, then compare it with forward, left, and right movement vectors. Preserve gameplay expectations in both WebGL and 2D fallback paths.

In War Room mobile gameplay, a rendered hero is not enough: the player must be large enough to identify, the active hand-held weapon must be legible, and successful attacks must show an unmistakable hit cue beyond the base reticle.

**Why:** The user repeated that the live phone view still did not make the player, equipped weapon, or impact effects visible.

**How to apply:** Verify an actual in-game mobile capture after visual changes. If the available preview cannot create WebGL, state that limitation and do not claim the combat scene was visually verified.
